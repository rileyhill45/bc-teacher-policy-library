01 Authority and Source Hierarchy

Authority: what governs a BC public school teacher

BC public-school teacher knowledge library | Version 2026-11-03

Scope: general guidance. For live disputes, verify current primary text and contact VSTA or other appropriate authority.

Referenced sources: S001, S002, S003, S004, S005, S006, S007, S024, S025, S026

Start with the correct question

Teacher questions often overlap education law, human rights law, occupational safety, a collective agreement, and a local administrative procedure. An answer drawn from only one source can be seriously incomplete.

Identify whether the question concerns: (a) an educational duty; (b) an employee right or working condition; (c) a student accommodation; (d) safety; (e) professional discipline; or (f) privacy. A single scenario can involve several at once.

Practical hierarchy for research

Read the relevant statute and regulation; then the current ministerial orders or mandatory Ministry policy; then the 2025-2029 provincial agreement and applicable Vancouver local language; then applicable VSB board policies and administrative procedures; then interpretive guidance. This is a research sequence, not an unconditional legal ranking: conflicting clauses require actual legal analysis.

The BC Human Rights Code contains an express precedence clause. Labour law and collective agreement language can create enforceable contractual rights that are not displaced by a general school-level preference. Do not treat a VSB web guideline as if it overrode a statute or collective agreement.

Current collective agreement warning

The VSTA explicitly says that its 2013-2019 agreement is the most recent consolidated (merged) copy accessible through its site. To know the current rule, consult that base plus 2019-2022, 2022-2025, and 2025-2029 provincial and local changes, including restored language where applicable.

The provincial 2025-2029 collective agreement was ratified in March 2026 and covers July 1, 2025 to June 30, 2029. Do not rely solely on a 2013-2019 PDF to answer a current employment question.

How to answer with confidence

Quote or identify the controlling section/article, state whether the source is current, separate the direct rule from reasonable interpretation, and explain any outstanding factual conditions. A district-specific answer must cite district-specific sources.

When the actual current collective agreement wording is not in this library, answer that the source index exists but the precise clause has not been verified. Refer the employee to the VSTA for interpretation, especially before discipline, refusal of instructions, or grievance deadlines.

02 Teacher Duties and Principal Authority

Teacher duties, principal authority, and required meetings

BC public-school teacher knowledge library | Version 2026-11-03

Scope: general guidance. For live disputes, verify current primary text and contact VSTA or other appropriate authority.

Referenced sources: S001, S002, S006, S007, S008, S013

Teacher duties under School Regulation section 4

Section 4(1) describes duties that include teaching and instructional assistance; student supervision as required; explaining school conduct rules; maintaining required records; encouraging regular attendance; evaluating and reporting; providing information as authorized; and advising the principal concerning class organization and placement of students with disabilities or diverse abilities.

Section 4(1)(k) requires attendance at meetings or conferences called by the principal or superintendent to discuss matters they consider necessary, unless the teacher is excused. This is a legal starting point; questions about when meetings occur, time compensation and workload must also be assessed against the applicable collective agreement.

Principal authority under section 5

Section 5(7) gives the principal responsibility for administering and supervising the school, including educational programs, class placements, timetables, learning activities, student evaluation and assessment, reporting, and records. Section 5 also deals with teacher evaluation and recommendations about discipline.

Consequently, a teacher has professional responsibilities and judgment, but not an absolute veto over all school-level assessment or programming directions. Conversely, a principal is not free to disregard statutes, protected student accommodation, or negotiated working conditions.

Disagreeing with an instruction

For a non-emergency disagreement, identify the exact instruction, confirm the source of authority, briefly record the professional concern, ask for the instruction in writing, and consult a union representative early. Do not advise a teacher to refuse a lawful management instruction based only on this library.

If the direction seems to compromise assessment integrity, professional ethics, student rights, or safety, flag those as distinct concerns. Occupational unsafe-work refusal follows a specific statutory process; educator misconduct reporting and child protection also have separate processes.

Meeting requests from parents

Parent conferences may be a necessary part of teachers' work, but a parent request alone is not identical to an instruction from the principal under section 4(1)(k). Scheduling, meeting format and expectations may be affected by school practice and the collective agreement.

The teacher may ask for a clear agenda, participating staff, desired outcome and adequate time to prepare. A parent cannot unilaterally set binding assessment standards or cancel the teacher's duty to assess curricular learning.

03 Collective Agreement Workload and Working Conditions

Collective agreement: workload, scheduling, preparation, and staffing

BC public-school teacher knowledge library | Version 2026-11-03

Scope: general guidance. For live disputes, verify current primary text and contact VSTA or other appropriate authority.

Referenced sources: S004, S005, S026, S027, S043, S053, S042

This is the controlling employment source for many questions

Hours, preparation time, assignment, staffing, class size/composition, supervision, salary, benefits, leaves, transfers, grievance timelines and related matters may depend on specific articles in the current provincial and local collective agreement. A public webpage summary is not the operative contract wording.

For Vancouver teachers, begin at VSTA's agreement index, open the consolidated base agreement, and apply every relevant later amendment. Check the 2025-2029 provincial text and 2025-2029 local settlement amendments. Use the union to validate interaction among provisions.

Common employee questions to route to the agreement

"Can administration require lunch supervision?" "Can I be assigned another class or preparation?" "Must I attend a meeting outside instructional hours?" "What happens if class composition exceeds agreed limits?" "Can my timetable or site assignment change?" These require clause-level verification, not a generic yes/no answer.

The Vancouver restoration LOU 17 includes historical language on workload, class size, inclusion, supervision and other matters. It should not be treated as self-sufficient proof of the exact current 2026 entitlement.

Class composition and support

A teacher may have reasonable concerns about workload and support for a class containing many students requiring adaptations. Those concerns can intersect educational duties under the School Regulation, contractual staffing terms, workplace safety and student accommodation obligations.

The correct response is to identify the current staffing/composition article, document the concern using non-identifying information where possible, ask for a support plan, and consult the staff representative. Avoid promising a fixed class-size entitlement until the current clause is checked.

Pay and leaves

Salary placement, increments, sick leave, maternity/parental leaves, benefits and professional development provisions must be checked in the current agreement and, where applicable, minimum statutory requirements. The Employment Standards Act has special rules for collective agreements; it is not universally inapplicable to unionized teachers.

The library deliberately does not provide specific wage rates, exact preparation minutes, or all contractual deadlines because they are amendment-sensitive. The underlying current agreement should be uploaded separately if copying is authorized by the employer or union.

04 Evaluation Discipline Grievances and Representation

Teacher evaluation, discipline, grievances, and union support

BC public-school teacher knowledge library | Version 2026-11-03

Scope: general guidance. For live disputes, verify current primary text and contact VSTA or other appropriate authority.

Referenced sources: S002, S003, S004, S005, S011, S025, S042

Teacher evaluation

School Regulation section 5 permits authorized administrators to report on a teacher's work and requires copies of certain reports to be delivered to the teacher. VSB Administrative Procedure 423 identifies teacher evaluation criteria and directs readers to collective agreement Article C.3 for the procedure.

Before answering whether an evaluation is permissible or procedurally correct, examine the current collective agreement (including any revised evaluation clause) and the specifics of the notice, observation, report and response process.

Discipline and representation

A workplace concern may be a supervisory conversation, a formal investigation, performance management, or potential discipline. The procedural rights and available remedies are different in each case. Do not assume every difficult meeting is disciplinary; equally, do not assume a meeting cannot affect employment.

An employee who reasonably anticipates discipline should contact the VSTA for advice and representation promptly. They can ask what the meeting concerns, request documents, preserve records, and avoid agreeing to findings or waiving rights without advice. Whether a representative must attend depends on the applicable contract and facts.

Grievance route

The Labour Relations Code provides a framework for interpreting and enforcing collective agreements through grievance/arbitration processes. An individual employee should normally work through their bargaining agent rather than assume they can file an employment grievance personally on their own terms.

Many grievance rights have strict contractual time limits. The agent must never invent a deadline; it should flag urgency and direct the teacher to their union representative immediately.

Professional regulation is separate

A complaint to the Commissioner for Teacher Regulation concerns educator certification/professional conduct and is distinct from an employer's disciplinary process. The Teachers Act creates its own reporting and investigative scheme.

If allegations involve student harm, child protection, criminal conduct or unsafe practices, direct the teacher to the specific statutory process as well as to their union for employment consequences.

05 Curriculum Professional Judgment and Assessment

Curriculum requirements and the limits of assessment discretion

BC public-school teacher knowledge library | Version 2026-11-03

Scope: general guidance. For live disputes, verify current primary text and contact VSTA or other appropriate authority.

Referenced sources: S001, S002, S008, S015, S017, S018, S019

What counts as a learning standard

The provincial curriculum uses a Know-Do-Understand model. Content lists required knowledge; Curricular Competencies specify subject-related skills and processes; Big Ideas describe broad conceptual understanding. Each course's official curriculum is the starting point for judging what students should know and be able to do.

Teachers generally select instructional examples, tasks and assessment methods, subject to governing law, mandated policy, student accommodation, and school procedures. This is professional judgment, not authority to disregard required standards.

Evidence versus assignments

Not every individual worksheet, quiz or activity is itself a mandated learning standard. Multiple standards can be demonstrated in one assessment; multiple assessment methods may be appropriate for diverse learners. Assessment is about defensible evidence of the intended learning, not necessarily reconstructing every missed task.

VSB AP 360 section 6.1 directs use of multiple methods of assessment; section 6.2 says methods should fit the learning objective; section 6.9 says judgments should be based on relevant evidence. Its section 6.12 separates achievement from effort and attitude.

How far does teacher discretion extend?

A teacher can usually explain why an exam, oral response, portfolio or analysis assignment does or does not provide sufficient evidence of particular standards. An accommodation might change format without changing the learning target. But a principal has supervisory responsibility for the school assessment program, and inaccessible tasks may require further accommodation.

A claim that the teacher alone has the final word on academic equivalence would overstate the legal position. A claim that parents can simply require any substitute assessment would also be unsupported.

Practical test of an alternative assessment

Ask: Which specific Content standards are measured? Which Curricular Competencies? What evidence will the student actually produce? What access barrier is being addressed? Does the change alter the way the student demonstrates the standard or the standard itself? How will scoring be fair and consistently documented?

Illustration: a short conversation could validly measure historical explanation, but might be insufficient to assess close comparison of primary sources unless the student is actually asked to analyze those sources. This is an example of professional reasoning, not a legal rule about oral tests.

06 Reporting Grades and Insufficient Evidence

Grade 9 versus Grade 10 reporting and Insufficient Evidence (IE)

BC public-school teacher knowledge library | Version 2026-11-03

Scope: general guidance. For live disputes, verify current primary text and contact VSTA or other appropriate authority.

Referenced sources: S002, S008, S009, S015, S016

Always identify the grade

The BC reporting framework differs by grade band. In Grades K-9 the provincial proficiency scale is used for learning standard achievement; Grades 10-12 use letter grades and percentages for courses. Grades 9 and 10 must not be handled as if their final reporting rules were identical.

The latest Ministry policy and applicable orders control the current reporting requirements. Check district systems and the most recent current Ministry version before entering a formal report.

Meaning of insufficient evidence

IE is a formal indicator for situations where the school lacks sufficient evidence to issue an accurate evaluation. It is not simply a zero, a punishment for absence, or a convenient way to avoid professional judgment. Under the Ministry policy, when IE is assigned, teachers must identify what prevents evidence collection, a plan of action, and a conversion timeline.

The Ministry policy includes limits on how long IE may remain, including conversion within 12 months. The guidelines caution that for a student who has missed most of a course, repeating the course may be more appropriate than treating IE as a simple short extension.

Non-submission and grading

Where a student has submitted little work, first identify what valid evidence already exists (including observations, performances and other assessment records), what learning standards remain unassessed, which accommodations are required, and what opportunity can reasonably be offered.

Do not invent a province-wide rule that a student automatically receives a failing grade after X missed assignments or that a teacher must provide a new assessment for every task. Use provincial policy, school procedure, the individual learning plan, and professional judgment.

Conflicting local and provincial reporting documents

VSB AP 361 is older and its displayed schedule references a reporting option that may not match the current School Regulation and K-12 Student Reporting Policy. For the number and form of reports, prefer current provincial regulation and Ministry orders; ask district staff which locally updated procedure applies.

Avoid having the agent quote older VSB reporting counts without checking this conflict. This knowledge library notes the issue rather than silently resolving it.

07 IEPs Accommodations and Disability Rights

IEPs, adapted assessments, accessibility, and disability accommodation

BC public-school teacher knowledge library | Version 2026-11-03

Scope: general guidance. For live disputes, verify current primary text and contact VSTA or other appropriate authority.

Referenced sources: S019, S020, S021, S024, S049, S051, S052, S008

Legal sources must be separated

Ministerial Order M638/95 sets requirements relating to individual education plans and is a binding order directed at school boards. Ministry Inclusive Education policy and manuals explain implementation. The Human Rights Code is separate legislation that applies to the provision of educational services, including disability discrimination and reasonable accommodation.

An IEP may describe adapted materials, teaching methods or assessment methods, support services, and in some cases individualized learning outcomes. Do not assume all IEPs modify prescribed provincial learning standards; distinguish accommodations/adaptations from formally modified learning expectations.

Following an IEP is important but not always exhaustive

IEP provisions should be implemented, but disability-related accommodation needs can evolve or may not have been fully captured in the written plan. The school, teacher and support team should address barriers; do not tell an employee that following the listed accommodations automatically discharges every Human Rights Code obligation.

Nor should the agent tell a teacher that any parent-suggested change is necessarily required. Decisions depend on accessibility, the legitimate educational aim, individual need, and whether another effective accommodation exists.

Fair assessment that still measures learning

A reasonable adapted assessment might include accessible reading format, assistive technology, additional time, chunked instructions, oral responses, smaller setting, or modified presentation of questions, depending on the needs and standards. The specific list is illustrative, not an automatic legal entitlement for every student.

Where the accommodation changes only the output mode, the teacher still needs evidence of the relevant standard. Where individual learning outcomes have formally been modified, assess and report in accordance with applicable policy on individualized learning goals.

Addressing disagreement or workload

Where parent, administration and teacher disagree, ask for the precise unmet barrier, the proposed alternative, the curricular target, and the support required. Clarify who is coordinating the IEP and who can authorize a change in plan. Document the educational reasoning without disclosing student details unnecessarily.

Potential complaints about denial of accommodation should be reviewed through district processes and, if necessary, specialized human rights or legal advice. Teachers also can seek union guidance when the workload or direction affects employment rights.

08 Attendance Absence Late Work and Catchup

Attendance, missed assessments, deadlines and catch-up planning

BC public-school teacher knowledge library | Version 2026-11-03

Scope: general guidance. For live disputes, verify current primary text and contact VSTA or other appropriate authority.

Referenced sources: S002, S010, S014, S015, S016, S008, S019

Recording and encouraging attendance

School Regulation section 4 includes encouraging regular attendance and maintaining records. VSB AP 330 makes attendance recording a shared process and expects teachers to record each secondary-school period in the student information system. AP 307 also references teacher attendance data duties.

An attendance problem should be documented and escalated through school systems; it is not solely an individual teacher's responsibility to resolve complex barriers to attendance.

Missed work and assessment opportunity

A teacher may reasonably set assignment and catch-up deadlines as part of managing assessment, but the library does not identify a blanket province-wide rule establishing one permissible deadline or an automatic right to refuse all late work. IEP requirements, disability accommodation, other protected grounds, school procedures and administrator direction can alter what is reasonable.

Similarly, a student returning after a long absence does not automatically establish a right to an individually recreated version of every missed worksheet or test. The educational question is whether sufficient evidence can be collected about the required learning standards in an accessible, fair process.

A practical documented catch-up plan

State the curricular areas still requiring evidence; inventory any work already completed; propose a limited number of appropriately adapted comprehensive assessments; name agreed supports, location, due dates, communication plan and what happens if evidence is still insufficient. Ensure students and parents understand the plan.

Bring the school-based team in when attendance barriers, disability-related needs, safeguarding, or course viability are involved. School administrators may direct modifications to scheduling or implementation.

If the student does not provide evidence

Follow the K-12 Student Reporting Policy for IE and its required plan/timeline, or consider other grade outcomes only where policy supports them. Distinguish lack of evidence from demonstrated poor achievement.

Never direct staff to infer that a student has mastered standards simply because of an IEP, or to assign a grade that is not supported by available evidence.

09 Student Safety Child Protection and Professional Conduct

Student safeguarding, educator duties to report, and professional standards

BC public-school teacher knowledge library | Version 2026-11-03

Scope: general guidance. For live disputes, verify current primary text and contact VSTA or other appropriate authority.

Referenced sources: S002, S003, S022, S023, S038, S039, S050

Child protection: mandatory duty

Under section 14 of the Child, Family and Community Service Act, a person who has reason to believe a child needs protection has a duty to report promptly to an appropriate child protection authority. The provincial reporting page provides the current 24-hour reporting telephone number. In immediate danger call emergency services.

Do not instruct a teacher to investigate independently before reporting, or to treat telling only a principal as necessarily satisfying the statutory duty. Follow the actual reporting statute and Ministry instructions.

Misconduct by another certified educator

Teachers Act section 38 imposes a distinct written, signed duty to report if an authorized person has reason to believe another authorized person engaged in conduct involving physical harm, sexual abuse/exploitation, or significant emotional harm to a student. The exact statutory conditions matter.

This report to teacher regulation authorities is not interchangeable with reporting concerns about a child to child protection. Circumstances can trigger more than one reporting duty.

Professional standards and boundaries

BC's professional standards guide practice and conduct; certification and discipline are governed by the Teachers Act and related regulation. Questions about student interactions, professional competence, confidentiality or conflicts should use those standards, applicable VSB policy and the current collective agreement.

Allegations involving criminal behaviour, child safety, or regulated professional misconduct should not be handled as routine disagreements over classroom procedure.

Criminal record requirements

The Criminal Records Review Act establishes screening obligations for employees in relevant positions and provisions concerning certain new charges or convictions. For any individual situation refer directly to the statute and employer's process; do not assume every charge or police interaction has identical reporting treatment.

10 Privacy Student Records and AI Tools

Privacy, student information, AI tools, and records retention

BC public-school teacher knowledge library | Version 2026-11-03

Scope: general guidance. For live disputes, verify current primary text and contact VSTA or other appropriate authority.

Referenced sources: S031, S032, S033, S034, S014, S054

Public-school privacy framework

BC boards of education are public bodies under the Freedom of Information and Protection of Privacy Act (FOIPPA), which regulates collection, use and disclosure of personal information. Employee emails, student records, educational assessments and IEP-related materials can contain personal information that needs appropriate protection.

The agent must never encourage teachers to paste student names, PEN numbers, diagnosis details, original assignments or disciplinary records into a third-party AI tool without district authorization and an appropriate lawful basis.

VSB-specific AI direction issued May 2026

VSB's AI Guidelines for Educators state that staff should use VSB-approved AI tools, fact-check AI outputs, protect student privacy, and avoid submitting identifiable student data or original student work without clear permission. The guidelines encourage transparency and accessibility.

A private SharePoint library about public employee policies need not contain any student-specific information or HR case records. It should contain only public source material and original general guidance.

Access, disclosure, and retention

A parent's or student's request for records may raise entitlement and redaction questions; route requests through approved VSB records and privacy procedures instead of informal email forwarding. FOIPPA governs public-body information access and privacy, subject to its exceptions.

Do not promise confidentiality more broadly than the law provides. Respect any retention schedules, district approved storage location, permissions and access logs.

Using AI for employee-rights guidance

Only upload documents with appropriate permission and check that the Microsoft tenant and agent permissions are configured as intended. Do not rely on the word "private" alone: a SharePoint site can expose information to everyone granted access to it.

Any agent answers about grievances, discipline or accommodation should say it provides general information and encourage union/legal advice for fact-specific rights. This is especially important when deadlines apply.

11 Workplace Safety Harassment and Accommodation

Workplace safety, bullying and harassment, and employee accommodations

BC public-school teacher knowledge library | Version 2026-11-03

Scope: general guidance. For live disputes, verify current primary text and contact VSTA or other appropriate authority.

Referenced sources: S024, S028, S029, S030, S047, S012, S051, S042

Safety obligations and rights

The Workers Compensation Act and Occupational Health and Safety Regulation establish duties for employers, supervisors and workers. Workers have obligations to report hazards and take reasonable care. Employers have duties to provide safe conditions and respond to recognized risks.

WorkSafeBC publishes the formal procedure for refusal of unsafe work. A worker who believes work presents an undue hazard must use that process, beginning with immediate reporting to an employer or supervisor. Do not invent a general right to walk away from all disagreeable work.

Workplace bullying and harassment

WorkSafeBC recognizes bullying/harassment as a safety issue and expects workplace prevention and complaint-response procedures. However, reasonable management direction is not automatically bullying or harassment simply because it is unwelcome.

VSB AP 172 addresses respectful workplace conduct and notes that relevant collective-agreement processes may take precedence. Employees should follow applicable reporting channels and ask the union about contract-specific steps.

Employee disability accommodations

BC Human Rights Code section 13 protects against discrimination in employment and may require individualized accommodation to the point of undue hardship. The employer and employee each have relevant roles in identifying needs and effective measures. Medical information should be treated as sensitive and provided through approved processes.

Do not confuse a teacher's own workplace accommodation rights with their separate duties to accommodate students under educational-service rules. Both may apply at once.

Danger, violence and urgent cases

Immediate threats should be directed to emergency or established school safety procedures. Document concrete hazards and incidents factually; avoid broad assumptions about students with disabilities or diverse abilities.

A teacher facing unsafe conditions, ongoing harassment, or a health-related work restriction should contact the appropriate district safety officer and VSTA promptly.

14 Public Interest Disclosure and Speaking Up

Whistleblowing, complaints, protected disclosures and union routes

BC public-school teacher knowledge library | Version 2026-11-03

Scope: general guidance. For live disputes, verify current primary text and contact VSTA or other appropriate authority.

Referenced sources: S035, S036, S037, S025, S042, S012

Different kinds of complaint

A routine disagreement over workload or assessment direction is normally an employment, supervisory or grievance issue, not automatically a whistleblower disclosure. Workplace harassment may follow a separate OHS/contractual path. Concerns about child abuse or certified educator harm have their own reporting laws.

The Public Interest Disclosure Act (PIDA) addresses defined categories of serious public-sector wrongdoing. Using the wrong procedure can delay resolution and may not provide the protections a teacher expects.

VSB and PIDA

VSB operates a PIDA disclosure process for current and former employees, including reporting to the designated district recipient or the BC Ombudsperson under the applicable rules. VSB lists serious legal wrongdoing, substantial danger, serious misuse of public assets/funds and gross/systemic mismanagement among eligible matters.

A disclosure should be based on factual information. Avoid adding identifiable student information to the policy agent; use the official protected reporting channel where needed.

Union role

A teacher who feels pressured to compromise assessment evidence, is facing discipline, or believes the employer has breached the collective agreement should contact their VSTA representative promptly. A grievance is not the same as a PIDA report.

The agent should never advise that a teacher can "ignore administration" without assessing legal and contractual obligations. Urgent safety and mandated child-protection reporting are separate cases with specific procedures.

15 Questions the Agent Should Answer Carefully

High-risk teacher questions: issue spotting and safe answers

BC public-school teacher knowledge library | Version 2026-11-03

Scope: general guidance. For live disputes, verify current primary text and contact VSTA or other appropriate authority.

Referenced sources: S002, S005, S008, S010, S015, S021, S024, S025, S030, S042

"Can I set a hard deadline for an adapted assessment?"

A teacher can propose reasonable assessment deadlines. There is no universal rule in this library granting absolute final authority over all deadlines, and there may be required accommodations or principal directions. Verify the IEP, student situation, VSB procedure, and current reporting policy.

The agent should suggest a documented, reasonable plan and avoid a categorical "yes, always" or "no, never."

"Must I make up every missed task?"

A key distinction is every original assignment versus sufficient evidence of the prescribed learning standards. Comprehensive assessments may be legitimate if accessible, educationally valid and consistent with school direction.

Absence alone does not prove lack of learning; nor does an IEP mean the student has demonstrated the standards. An IE designation must follow the formal reporting requirements.

"Can the principal order an assessment change?"

The principal supervises the school assessment program under School Regulation section 5(7), while the teacher has responsibility for evaluating and reporting under section 4 and VSB AP 360 emphasizes relevant evidence. The interaction is not a simple unrestricted veto for either side.

Ask for direction and rationale in writing, identify the standards/evidence at issue, and consult VSTA if the direction raises contractual, ethical or employment concerns.

"Do I have to attend a parent meeting?"

A meeting called by the principal or superintendent for a matter they consider necessary engages section 4(1)(k) unless excused; whether timing and workload implicate contractual rights requires current clause review. A direct invitation from a parent does not automatically carry the same statutory status.

Suggest asking for an agenda, appropriate support personnel and confirmation of expectations, not unilateral refusal.

"Can a teacher fail a student with an IEP?"

Having an IEP neither automatically determines a grade nor eliminates the need to assess the appropriate learning outcomes. The correct answer depends on any modified outcomes, assessment evidence, required accommodations, grade-band reporting and other relevant policy.

Never tell the user to assign a grade that does not reflect the available evidence; seek learning-support and administrator direction where needed.

"Do I have to follow a management instruction that I believe is wrong?"

Most workplace directions should be handled by clarifying and documenting, then seeking union advice before refusing; there are special rules for unsafe work, mandatory reporting, and potentially unlawful directions.

The agent should not provide definitive instructions to refuse, resign, or sign a disciplinary document based on general policy notes alone.
